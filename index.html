Grammar I & II

Grammar I: Possessive Adjectives (My, Your, His, Her)

Possessive adjectives show who something belongs to. In First Primary, we use My (for I), Your (for You), His (for a boy/man), and Her (for a girl/woman) to talk about our body parts and physical features!

I ➔ My

"My nose is small."

You ➔ Your

"Your eyes are green."

He ➔ His

"His hands are big."

She ➔ Her

"Her hair is black."

Grammar II: Ability with "Can"

We use the modal verb can to talk about abilities (things we are able to do). To ask questions, we put Can at the beginning!

Affirmative & Negative Statements

  • I can draw. / She can run.
  • No, he cannot (can't) walk.

Questions and Short Answers

  • Can you draw? ➔ Yes, I can.
  • Can the father cook? ➔ Yes, he can. / No, he can't.

Solved Examples (15 Total: 5 Basic, 5 Intermediate, 5 Advanced)

Review these step-by-step examples based on NatGeo Our World 1.

⭐ Basic Level Examples (Possessive Adjectives & Basic Can)

Example 1 (Basic)

Sentence: "____ nose is small." (Subject: I)

Solution: My. Reason: The pronoun 'I' pairs with the possessive adjective 'My'.

Example 2 (Basic)

Sentence: "____ eyes are green." (Subject: You)

Solution: Your. Reason: The pronoun 'You' matches with 'Your'.

Example 3 (Basic)

Sentence: "____ hands are small." (Subject: He / Grandfather)

Solution: His. Reason: 'He' refers to a male, so we use 'His'.

Example 4 (Basic)

Sentence: "____ mouth is red." (Subject: She / Girl)

Solution: Her. Reason: 'She' refers to a female, so we use 'Her'.

Example 5 (Basic)

Question: "Can you draw?" ➔ Answer: "Yes, I ______."

Solution: can. Reason: Affirmative short answers use the modal 'can'.

⭐⭐ Intermediate Level Examples (Contextual Body Parts & Questions)

Example 6 (Intermediate)

Look at the grandfather: "He is tall. ______ feet are big."

Solution: His. Reason: Referring to the grandfather (male), we use 'His'.

Example 7 (Intermediate)

Look at the girl: "She is short. ______ legs are short."

Solution: Her. Reason: Referring to the girl (female), we use 'Her'.

Example 8 (Intermediate)

Question: "Can the father cook?" ➔ Short Answer: "Yes, he ______."

Solution: can. Reason: Confirming ability for the father in the affirmative.

Example 9 (Intermediate)

Question: "Can the baby sister cook?" ➔ Short Answer: "No, she ______."

Solution: can't (cannot). Reason: Negative short answer for a baby's inability to cook.

Example 10 (Intermediate)

Statement: "No. He ______ walk." (Looking at a baby in a stroller)

Solution: can't. Reason: Expressing lack of physical ability.

⭐⭐⭐ Advanced Level Examples (Combined Grammar & Structural Agreement)

Example 11 (Advanced)

Complete: "Look at her head. ______ hair is black and ______ mouth is small."

Solution: Her / Her. Reason: Both describe features belonging to the same female subject ('her').

Example 12 (Advanced)

Complete: "Look at his head. ______ hair is white and ______ nose is big."

Solution: His / His. Reason: Both describe features belonging to the same male subject ('his').

Example 13 (Advanced)

Form the question: "______ the brother clean?" (Answer: "Yes, he can.")

Solution: Can. Reason: Ability questions with 'can' always begin with the modal verb capitalized at the start of the sentence.

Example 14 (Advanced)

Contrast: "He is tall, but ______ arms are short. ______ sister can jump high."

Solution: his / His (or Her). Reason: Combining possessive pronouns in descriptive narrative contexts.

Example 15 (Advanced)

Dialogue: "Can she watch TV?" ➔ "Yes, ______ ______."

Solution: she can. Reason: Short affirmative answers mirror the pronoun ('she') and the modal ('can').

⭐ Basic Level Exercises

1. Complete with My or Your: "______ nose is small." (Pointing to yourself)

Solution: My. (Because you are talking about yourself).

2. Complete with My or Your: "______ eyes are green." (Talking to a friend)

Solution: Your. (Because you are talking directly to someone else).

3. Complete with His or Her: "______ hands are small." (Talking about a girl)

Solution: Her. (Female possessive adjective).

4. Complete with His or Her: "______ mouth is red." (Talking about a boy)

Solution: His. (Male possessive adjective).

5. Answer the question: "Can you draw?" ➔ "Yes, I ______."

Solution: can. (Affirmative short answer).

⭐⭐ Intermediate Level Exercises

6. Choose Her / His: "He is tall. ______ feet are big."

Solution: His. (Refers to the grandfather/male).

7. Choose Her / His: "She is short. ______ hands are small."

Solution: Her. (Refers to the girl).

8. Complete: "No. He ______ walk." (Baby in stroller)

Solution: can't (cannot). (Negative ability).

9. Complete question: "______ his sister jump?"

Solution: Can. (Questions start with Can).

10. Complete short answer: "Can the father cook?" ➔ "Yes, he ______."

Solution: can. (Affirmative short answer).

⭐⭐⭐ Advanced Level Exercises

11. Write about the girl: "She is short. ______ legs are short and ______ arms are short."

Solution: Her / Her. (Both blanks refer to the girl).

12. Write about the grandfather: "He is tall. ______ feet are big and ______ hands are big."

Solution: His / His. (Both blanks refer to the grandfather).

13. Complete the question & answer: "______ the baby sister cook?" ➔ "No, she ______."

Solution: Can / can't. (Interrogative modal + negative short answer).

14. Personalize: "______ hair is black, and ______ nose is small." (About yourself)

Solution: My / My. (First-person possessive adjectives).

15. Complete dialogue: "Can she watch TV?" ➔ "Yes, ______ ______."

Solution: she can. (Subject pronoun + modal verb).

Critical Reflection & Logical Thinking

Discuss these questions with your teacher or classmates in class.

Question 1: Identity & Belonging

Why do we use different words like my, your, his, and her when talking about our bodies? How does this help us describe our friends at Colegio Montano correctly?

Question 2: Abilities & Growth

Think about when you were a baby: you couldn't walk or talk. Now, in First Primary, what are some amazing new things you can do that you couldn't do before?
